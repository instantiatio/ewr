# Working with iEWR: Skills for Engineers and Team Leads {#advanced-use}

Engineers give iEWR work within their area of responsibility, take part in key decisions, and assess the outcome. Engineering team leads do the same for more complex systems, where decisions affect one another and the goal may become clearer as the work progresses.

Develop these skills on your own tasks. Start by describing in plain language what you need, who needs it, and what they will use it for. If the outcome is still unclear, describe the problem you are facing.

## Work with the system as a whole {#work-with-the-system-as-a-whole}

In [System Formation](/ewr/eng/foundations/system-formation/), what to do next depends on the system you need, what already exists, and what is still missing for it to be useful. A system can be an application, a product, a document, or a way for a team to work. Plans and methods can change as you learn more.

A complaint about a request form, for example, may point to an interface defect, a processing rule, or a misunderstanding of the form's purpose. Ask the agent to investigate the cause and its relation to the whole system so it can identify the necessary change.

To decide what to do next, the agent needs relevant information about the system's purpose, environment, current state, decisions, and dependencies. Share the materials you have and communicate new decisions. The agent builds the context for the work and records the reasoning and results in the project so you can return to them.

## Practical engineering skills {#practical-engineering-skills}

| Skill | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **Get started** | Open the project in your agent environment and describe a real task in plain language. |
| **Explain the outcome you need** | Say what you are creating or changing, who needs it, and how they will use it. If this is still unclear, describe the problem. |
| **Share the inputs** | Provide available materials, current decisions, and constraints. Flag missing information. |
| **Establish the current state** | Ask the agent what already exists, which properties have been verified, and what remains unclear. |
| **Delegate an outcome** | Describe the expected result and the bounds of independent action. Let the agent choose how to do the work. |
| **Keep the context current** | Share new facts, requirements, and decisions. Ask the agent to revisit any work they affect. |
| **Make decisions** | Consider the options that matter and their consequences. Grant permission within your authority. |
| **Ask for the reasoning** | Ask why the agent proposes an action, what facts support it, and what still needs checking. |
| **Assess fitness for use** | Compare the properties you need with what the checks show and the conditions in which the result will be used. |
| **Get discrepancies fixed** | Show where the result falls short and ask the agent to identify and carry out the necessary changes. |
| **Resume after a break** | Work with the agent to establish which decisions still apply, what changed, and which questions remain open. |

**What proficiency looks like.** You can complete a real task, explain the result's purpose and what supports its use, identify what remains unknown, and get a specific discrepancy fixed.

## Skills for engineering team leads {#the-engineering-team-leaders-own-work}

Leads need the same skills, along with the ability to preserve the purpose and coherence of a system when the work spans several connected parts or important conditions are still unknown.

| Skill | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **Set the intent for the whole system** | Explain its purpose, intended use, and what would make the outcome fit for use. |
| **Delegate a complex outcome** | Assign work that requires several connected contributions and let the agent choose the sequence. |
| **Investigate uncertainty and opportunities** | Test assumptions about needs and value before using them to choose a direction. |
| **Keep decisions coherent** | Check proposed changes against the system's purpose, architecture, environment, dependencies, and verified properties. |
| **Set the mandate** | Define permitted actions and consequences, when to stop, and which decisions the agent must bring back to you. |
| **Assess significant alternatives** | Compare options by benefit, full cost, risk, and consequences for the whole system. |
| **Find unmet dependencies** | Identify any knowledge, decision, test, resource, permission, or other participant's contribution still needed. |
| **Revisit the direction** | Reconsider the goal, criteria, and decisions as circumstances change. Retain results that are still supported by the evidence. |
| **Check the system as a whole** | Check how its parts interact and whether the whole system is fit for its intended use. |
| **Assess value in use** | Look at what the result does for its recipients in practice. Completed work and successful checks are different from evidence of that benefit. |
| **Know when the work is sufficient** | Finish when the outcome is in place, the necessary checks are complete, and obligations are met. Reconsider any remaining items from the old plan. |

**What proficiency looks like.** You can delegate a complex or loosely defined task, hold on to its purpose and important dependencies, make the necessary decisions, and assess the whole system. When conditions change, you can adjust the direction while preserving the agent's independence within agreed bounds.

### Assign a complex result {#assign-a-complex-result}

> We need a working arrangement for transferring product support to another team. Investigate the current situation, compare options, and prepare a coherent set of roles, access, knowledge, interactions, and readiness checks. Carry out preparation and analysis independently. Bring decisions about participants, authority, and changes to live systems back to me. Show what is still missing for the transition.

## How to develop these skills {#assess-your-progress}

1. **Choose a real task.** Explain what you need and how it will be used.
2. **Share the information you have.** Ask the agent what already exists, what is unknown, and what needs doing now.
3. **Set the bounds of independent action.** Decide which decisions you will keep and which actions the agent can take.
4. **Inspect the outcome.** What changed? What has been verified? Can the intended recipient use the result?
5. **Continue or finish.** If you find a discrepancy, assign the follow-up work. If the result is sufficient, move on to another task.

**The measure of proficiency is being able to obtain and assess fit-for-use results with AI as conditions change**, rather than knowing the terminology or counting completed tasks.

## The engineering team as a system {#when-a-team-is-involved}

An engineering team creates or changes a target system. Within its mandate, the team can also change its own composition, division of work, and ways of collaborating.

Shared project context helps participants see how decisions connect. Working together also requires agreement on goals, contributions, responsibilities, and authority. Participants need to know whose work they depend on, who receives their results, and who integrates them.
